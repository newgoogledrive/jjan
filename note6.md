# note 6 (jewel update 2)

**Jewel Updates — about a month after the first team went missing**

RoTU has set up a small headquarters in the old coast survey office. It is
close enough to coordinate the investigation, but not right next to Jewel 2.
We have a records room, a radio desk, and a place to review the plans without
having to carry everything back and forth.

The office still has the old coast survey maps on the walls. We cleared one
table for the Harbor Lantern plans and another for equipment checks. The radio
desk is staffed whenever anyone is out at the site. We are keeping paper copies
of the check-in times in case the radio log is lost or changed.

We have made a logo for RoTU now. It will be used on the team's reports. It
helps keep the joint team's paperwork separate from the older JJAN and NARAO
files.

RoTU has started investigating the site. This time there are more precautions.
Nobody goes in alone, every visit has a check-in time, and another team stays
at the office monitoring the radio. We are keeping a clear route back out and
limiting how long anyone spends inside.

Before each visit, the team agrees on a route and writes down the rooms they
intend to enter. They carry a compass, measuring tape, chalk, lights, and two
radios. One radio stays with the person monitoring from outside. If the team
misses a check-in, nobody goes in after them without a new plan.

We are not treating this as an ordinary inspection. Three people disappeared
during the first reconstruction, and then the five-person team we sent went
missing. We are not going to repeat the same mistakes just to get a closer
look at a room.

For now the work is in short visits. Nobody is to explore alone or follow a
passage that is not on the plan. If a doorway or room does not match the
drawings, the team marks its position, records what they can from a safe
distance, and turns back.

## RoTU field report

The newest report is from the lower levels. The team entered before dawn and
reached the heart room shortly before 05:00. They had checked their route and
radio at each landing. All check-ins were received up to that point.

At 05:00 they saw the heart in the heart room. It beat once. The report says
there was no warning sound and no movement from the equipment described in the
old plans. The single beat matches the pulse reported in the connector room.

The room changed while they were there. The walls seemed farther apart than
they had been on entry, and the measurements no longer matched the plan. The
doorway they had used to enter was not where they expected it to be. The team
did not try to find out why. They followed the marked return route and got
out. The team is safe.

They compared notes at the office. Both people in the heart room recorded the
same beat time, but their measurements of the room differ. The person at the
radio desk heard a brief gap in the transmission at 05:01. The team says the
radio worked normally again before they reached the upper level.

They have not gone back in. We need to compare their notes, radio recording,
and measurements before deciding what to do next.

The old plans describe a machine in that room. The team says what they saw
did not look like a machine. They have not agreed on another description yet.

The connector room pulse is still reported at 05:00. We do not know if the
heart in the lower room is the source, or if both sounds are coming from
something else. The conduit between the rooms is still the only connection
shown in the plans.

## next

RoTU is checking the radio recording and comparing the room measurements with
the Harbor Lantern plans. NARAO is reviewing the change in the room dimensions.
JJAN is checking the old field reports for earlier mentions of the brief radio
gap.

No further entry into the lower levels until that review is finished. We are
not sure what the room will look like the next time someone opens the door.
